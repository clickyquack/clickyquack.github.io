Brief Overview and Theory of Change

There’s a strong argument to be made that shifting political will towards an international governance agreement for AI should be the top priority for mitigating existential risk from future AI systems right now, but currently, resources painting a clearer picture of how to most effectively do this are underprioritized and scattered. The purpose of this project is to create a central resource to help more people understand what helps the most with this, and to try to minimize friction for them to contribute to this work. It tries to answer overview questions like “What progress has been made so far?”, “What still needs to happen?”, “Given our evidence thus far, what would most likely make the most difference in getting that to happen?”, and helps the reader “zoom in” to specific parts to identify what they ought to prioritize, based on an understanding of how it fits into the big-picture goal. Most of the value is knowledge-mapping and providing better structure for finding external resources that apply to narrow topics; I expect relatively little original writing to be published on the project itself, other than insofar as it is in service of this goal (although readers are encouraged to submit new original works from e.g. their LessWrong or Substack posts to be included if they fill current gaps well, and I will probably write some of my own).

Future project developments

I’d like to eventually have a dedicated page(s) for a list of early-stage ideas for what to prioritize next, both for the project as a whole and for specific sub-sections, and a way to see the most recent additions/changes. Increasingly more pages will be broken into sub-maps and/or sub-pages insofar as this is helpful for the greater goal of the project. It’s sort of a constant work-in-progress, although I expect I will likely eventually decrease or even cease my efforts on this project as it helps me find higher-leverage ways to contribute directly.

Future content

Off the top of my head, some ideas for what I want to work on for this next, roughly in priority order:

  • Go into more detail on insider policy work, as I suspect this to be the most important current gap
  • Fitting more insights from “The current bottleneck is political will, not research” into the map
  • More on smaller/easier relevant policy changes, such as trying to answer “Why isn’t e.g. DNA screening mandated yet? What would best help change this? How does this tie into the larger goal of an international AI treaty?”
  • Evaluating the impact of different types of advocacy efforts and how they fit into the bigger picture, e.g. directly targeting lawmakers, collecting signatures on open statements, general public targeted stuff, etc.
  • Evaluating the impact of specific relevant orgs such as PauseAI and ControlAI, how they fit into the bigger picture, what they could be doing better, etc.
  • Something about the role grantmaking plays, evaluating where additional funding for this work would be most impactful, how to convince grantmakers to fund this work, etc.
  • More on the specifics of the role China is playing, where they currently stand, how to get them more on-board with an existential-risk mitigating AI treaty, etc.

Open collaboration?

In the spirit of “minimizing friction to contribute”, I’d like to potentially try giving the project itself a Wikipedia-like open collaboration structure, where users start out only able to make and discuss proposals for improvements, then gain more permissions to make changes directly if they prove to be consistently high-quality. The potential upside seems high, and the cost of trying low; if it proves to be more trouble than it’s worth, I could simply discard it. If I do this I would need to phase out use of first-person pronouns in the project’s content. Also, open-collaboration aside, wiki or wiki-like software would be a good fit for this project’s goal.

Is this overkill for the purpose it’s trying to serve?

In theory, this could instead all live on a very long LessWrong or Substack post structured by its table of contents that I regularly update, sometimes writing new original posts to link to within it. Giving it a standalone website makes structuring navigation between sub-pages more flexible (e.g. with diagrams), makes it feel more like something to come back to, would make an open-collaboration structure work much more smoothly, etc. I don’t really think this is overkill, and even if this helps no one else it’s helping me. If you have any better ideas or think I’m missing something, let me know.
